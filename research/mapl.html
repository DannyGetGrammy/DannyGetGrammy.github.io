Improving LLM Collaboration via Multi-Agent Preference Learning

Preference-based optimization for multi-agent language-model collaboration, supported by reproducible model training and evaluation workflows.

Preference LearningLLM AgentsMulti-Agent Systems

Overview

Collaboration between language-model agents creates a difficult learning problem: it is often easier to compare the quality of two team outputs than to write a single reliable reward function. This work studies preference learning as a framework for optimizing multi-agent collaboration.

The paper introduces a multi-agent preference learning framework, with decentralized and centralized perspectives and two optimization approaches: MARL from feedback and multi-agent direct preference optimization.

My contributions

  • Built and debugged reproducible Linux, CUDA, and PyTorch training environments on GPU workstations and HPC systems.
  • Developed evaluation pipelines with subprocess execution, failure handling, structured logs, and repeatable configurations for multi-agent LLM benchmarks.
  • Investigated benchmark inconsistencies and corrected prompt/test mismatches in collaborative code-generation tasks.
  • Supported experiment execution and analysis for collaborative LLM learning.

Research directions

I am especially interested in how team-level supervision and preference feedback can help multiple models coordinate in tasks that cannot be reduced to simple per-agent rewards.
